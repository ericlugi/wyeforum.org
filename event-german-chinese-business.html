No. 03 Session 05 December 2025

Global Industry, Innovation, and Cross-Border Strategy

Mr. Wei joined us straight off a flight to talk about Germany's hidden champions, and what decades of leading between two business cultures actually teaches you.

The opening address before Mr. Wei's session, 05 December 2025.

Mr. Wei, Executive Vice President of Freudenberg's Automotive Sector and former CEO of NOK-Freudenberg China, joined our second session after landing from a flight, an early sign of the intellectual generosity that ran through the next ninety minutes. His subject was global industry, innovation, and cross-border strategy, drawn from a career spent operating between Germany and China.

The room ahead of the session. By this point, participation had grown more international, with new members joining from the United States.

A hidden champion, in its own words

Mr. Wei opened with Freudenberg itself, a company most participants had never had reason to know. He framed it through Germany's tradition of "hidden champions," firms that lead their industries globally while staying largely unknown outside them. A slide he shared quoted former German Chancellor Olaf Scholz describing Freudenberg as a model of what makes German industry strong, alongside the company's 2024 Family Company of the Year award.

From the session: Freudenberg introduced as one of Germany's hidden champions, with its 2024 Family Company of the Year recognition.

From there he walked through the shape of the company itself, four business areas spanning seals and vibration control, technical textiles and filtration, cleaning technologies, and a set of specialty divisions reaching into chemicals, medical products, and power systems. The range was the point. A single private company, built across materials most people never think about, had found a way to lead globally in each of them.

From the session: Freudenberg's business groups and areas, spanning seals, filtration, cleaning technology, and specialty materials.

Built across two business cultures

The more personal thread of the talk was Mr. Wei's own path, from sales-oriented roles early in his career to senior executive leadership, including his time as CEO of NOK-Freudenberg China. He returned often to adaptability, describing how operating between Germany and Asia demanded more than translation between two languages. It meant understanding two different ways of making decisions, managing risk, and building trust, and learning to move between them without losing either.

He spoke under the title "Innovating for a Pure Life," Freudenberg's own framing for its filtration and sustainability work, and used it to make a wider point about how innovation actually happens inside a large industrial company. Not as a single breakthrough, but as continuous reinvestment in research and development, paired with a willingness to restructure when a business line stops serving the company's direction.

Mr. Wei mid-discussion, under Freudenberg's "Innovating for a Pure Life" framing.

What competition actually rewards

Much of the discussion turned on mergers, acquisitions, and the shifting competitive position of firms within an industry. Mr. Wei was direct about the implication: success in global industry is rarely permanent. Companies that stop innovating fall behind, often without realizing it until the gap is already wide, while companies that identify where an industry is heading next tend to be the ones still standing a decade later.

That theme connected back to his own career in a way participants picked up on quickly. Corporate governance and cross-border strategy, the more abstract parts of the talk, became concrete the moment he traced them through decisions he had actually made. A student's question about how he handled disagreement between German and Chinese leadership teams led to one of the longer exchanges of the session.

Mid-discussion. The questions about leading across two business cultures drew some of the closest attention of the session.
Versatility and continuous learning matter more than any single piece of expertise, especially for anyone trying to operate across different business environments and cultural contexts. From Mr. Wei's remarks, paraphrased from the session

By the end, the conversation had moved well past Freudenberg as a company. It became a broader look at what leadership requires when a firm's success depends on operating credibly in markets that do not share a single set of assumptions about how business gets done.

What stayed with us

For a forum still finding its footing, having someone with Mr. Wei's actual cross-border leadership experience speak so candidly about adaptability and the limits of any one market's playbook was the kind of session that is hard to plan for and easy to recognize once it happens. It also marked a shift in who the Forum was reaching. New participants from outside the original group joined this session, a sign that the exchange the Forum set out to encourage was beginning to extend further than its first room.
